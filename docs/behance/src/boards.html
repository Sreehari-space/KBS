The counter

One hand on the scale. One hand on the phone. A queue behind.

A kirana counter is not a desk. Rice is being weighed while the next customer is already reading out their list, the last one is asking to put it on the khata, and the phone doing all of this is a ₹8,000 Android with two bars of signal on a good day.

Nothing in that sentence is a design detail. It is the entire specification. Every decision in this project was made by asking what the counter would do to it.

Network is optional

Not a degraded mode. There is no server, no login and no sync. Offline is the only state the app has ever been in.

Money is the interface

The running total is the largest thing on the screen and never leaves it. Every amount is an integer number of paise.

Two scripts, one counter

Tamil and English, switched from the header because staff change mid-shift — never buried in settings.

A half-built bill is sacred

The cart auto-saves as it is built. A dropped phone or a dead battery must not cost the shopkeeper the sale in progress.

The till at rest. Sixty-seven Tamil Nadu staples ship seeded, so the first bill can be rung up before anything is set up.

Scope

Principles

Four rules that settled every argument.

The counter sets the clock

If a decision costs the operator a second during a queue, it loses. Animation is capped at 260 ms and can be switched off entirely, because slow motion on a cheap Android is worse than none.

Money is typography, not text

Amounts are set in tabular numerals so columns align, grouped the Indian way, and the paise are stepped down so the rupees read first. The arithmetic behind them never touches a float.

Offline is the normal state

Being offline gets a quiet dot, never a banner. Banners are reserved for things that are actually wrong — storage that will not persist, a backup that has gone stale.

Anything touching money gets tested

Refunds, bill numbering, credit and the payment sheet are pure, tested modules rather than component state. A defect here is not a visual bug; it is a shop losing rupees.

A teal that survives sunlight, and one mint that never repeats.

Two colours were rejected by measurement

A lighter, friendlier teal measured 3.7:1 on white and failed for small text, so the brand hue is darker than it wants to be. The muted grey first drawn at #6B7A77 measured 4.08:1 and was lifted to #5F6E6B for 4.86:1. Neither change was visible in a mockup; both were visible in a contrast check.

The accent is a budget, not a palette

Mint appears once per screen, on the single action that finishes what the operator started — BILL, then Complete sale. Cards are separated by the gap between them rather than by borders or heavy shadows, so the only strong colour on the screen is the thing to press next.

Money

Rupees read first. Paise get out of the way.

₹1,24,560.00

Grouped the Indian way

Lakh grouping, not thousands. A shopkeeper reading 1,24,560 sees one lakh twenty-four thousand at a glance; the same digits grouped as 124,560 have to be counted.

Paise at 82%, dimmed

The decimals are set smaller and softer everywhere an amount appears. They still have to be there — a bill without them looks rounded — but they must not compete with the rupees.

Tabular, always

Every numeral is the same width, so a column of amounts lines up on the decimal without a table, and a total that changes does not make the layout twitch.

₹1,160.00

And underneath: no floats, ever

Money is stored and computed as an integer number of paise. Not a rupee float, not a decimal string. ₹0.1 + ₹0.2 is a rounding anecdote in most software; in a shop’s ledger it is a discrepancy the owner has to explain at closing time. Totals, tax, discounts, refunds and change all resolve in whole paise before anything is displayed.

Two scripts

Tamil is not a translation layer. It is the default.

The app boots in Tamil. English is the switch, and the switch sits in the header where a second staff member can reach it mid-shift, not three taps deep in settings.

விற்பனை பொருட்கள்

ஆவின் பால் · துவரம் பருப்பு · இட்லி அரிசி

Letter-spacing The tight tracking that makes Latin headings sit well breaks Tamil glyph clusters. Every display style resets it under :lang(ta) and gives the line more leading.
No uppercasing Tamil has no case. uppercase on a label is a silent no-op in one language and damage in the other, so it is gone from shared components.
Tanglish input Typing “ponni arisi” fills in பொன்னி அரிசி from an offline lexicon and a phonetic fallback. Nobody switches keyboards mid-queue.
Headline sizes Tamil sets a third wider in a taller script, so display sizes step down by language rather than wrapping a heading to five lines on a 360 px phone.

The same till, same code path. The receipt, the reports and the printer output are all translated too — not just the chrome.

The till

Everything within a thumb, and the total never moves.

Search, scan, speak Three ways in, side by side. Typing is fastest for a regular, the camera for anything with a barcode, the mic for hands that are busy with a scoop.
Quick items The shop’s own fastest sellers, chosen during setup and editable later. Price and stock on the face of each tile, so the answer to “how much?” never needs a tap.
The next bill number Shown before the sale as a peek, never an allocation. The real number is handed out inside the commit transaction, or two fast taps could share one.
Saved A quiet tick, not a toast. The cart is written to the device as it is built, so a dropped phone reopens on the same half-built bill.
The total bar Pinned to the bottom of the screen where the thumb already is, carrying the largest number in the app and the one mint action beside it.

One sale

Five moves from an empty screen to paper in a customer’s hand.

01 — FIND

Type, scan or speak. Results are ranked so the first one is usually right.

02 — WEIGH

Loose goods ask for a quantity. Packets skip this entirely and go straight in.

03 — CHECK

Lines, quantities and the total. Hold the whole cart if the customer goes back for one more thing.

04 — TAKE

Cash, UPI, card or khata — and any split of them. Note denominations, get the change back.

05 — GIVE

Print it, WhatsApp it, or hand the phone over. The bill is already saved either way.

Loose goods

A shop sells rice by the kilo and by the fifty rupees.

Half the transactions at a provision counter are not “one of something”. They are two kilos of idli rice, or a hundred rupees of groundnut oil — and the customer is as likely to name the money as the weight.

So the quantity sheet works in both directions. By weight, it multiplies up to a price. By amount, it divides down to a weight. The keypad is the same either way, and the presets underneath cover the quantities a kirana shop actually sells: 100 g, 250 g, half a kilo, one, two, five.

The numbers are big because the phone is being tapped at arm’s length with one hand while the other holds the scoop.

Packets don’t ask

A tile priced per piece adds one straight to the cart. Making a packet of biscuits go through a weight sheet would be a tax on the commonest tap in the app.

Stock stays honest

Quantities are decimal-safe and the stock is decremented in the same atomic write that commits the sale — never in a second step that could fail on its own.

Credit

Credit is not a way of paying. It is the absence of one.

Choosing khata names the customer and leaves the balance owing.

The ledger is append-only. Balances are derived, never edited.

The modelling mistake that cost a week

Khata — the running tab a regular customer settles at the end of the month — was first modelled as a fourth payment method sitting beside cash, UPI and card. It looks the same on screen. It is the opposite underneath: the other three reduce what is outstanding, and this one is what is outstanding.

Once that was fixed, a sale became a total with a list of payments against it and whatever remains going to a named customer. Split tenders — ₹200 cash now, the rest on the book — fell out of the model for free, and so did settling the book later without touching the original bill.

Append-only Every credit given and every rupee collected is an entry. Balances are computed from the entries, so no total can drift away from its history.
Immutable sales A committed bill is never edited. A mistake becomes a return with its own number, so the day’s takings still add up.

The bill

One bill. Four ways out of the phone.

The bill on screen is not a picture of a receipt — it is the receipt, torn edges and all, set in the same 58 mm measure the printer will use. What the shopkeeper checks on glass is exactly what the customer gets on paper.

Paper A 58 mm HTML layout for the browser’s own print path, for shops with a USB printer on a counter PC.
WhatsApp Plain text, monospaced and aligned by spaces, because that is what survives being pasted into a chat.
Image The same bill drawn to a canvas, for sharing where text formatting would collapse.
Thermal ESC/POS over Web Bluetooth — sent as a raster, because cheap thermal printers have no Tamil font in ROM. Sending characters prints boxes; sending pixels prints Tamil.

Every renderer reads one model. Adding a channel never means re-deriving totals, and a change to how tax is shown appears in all four at once.

Reports

A number the owner can act on, not a dashboard to admire.

Sales, bills, average bill and credit given — each against the previous period, each opening into the bills behind it.

Busiest hours Sales by hour across the whole period, so a shop can see when to have a second person behind the counter.
Best sellers By revenue, not by count — the ranking that tells the owner what to reorder first.
Profit, honestly Margin is reported only over lines with a recorded cost price. Treating a missing cost as zero would flatter the shop and make the number worthless, so uncosted revenue is shown separately instead.
Day close Expected cash against counted cash, with the difference stated plainly. The one screen that ends the working day.

Offline

There is no server. That is the feature, not the compromise.

Everything is local

Sales, stock, customers and settings live in IndexedDB on the phone. The shell is precached, so after installing, the app makes no network requests at all — there is nothing left to request.

One transaction, or none

Committing a sale writes the bill, the stock movements, the ledger entry and the bill counter in a single atomic transaction. There is no state in which a sale exists but the stock never moved.

Storage is finite, so say so

A bill is roughly a kilobyte. Five megabytes of quota is about six weeks of a busy shop — so the app watches its own quota and asks for a backup before it is in trouble, rather than after.

Trust is shown, not claimed

Being offline gets a small amber dot beside the screen name — it is the expected state, so it does not get a banner. Banners are spent only on things that are genuinely wrong: a browser that will not persist storage, a quota nearly full, a backup that has not been taken in a week. A backup restore is never silent; it says what it is about to replace, first.

The migration nobody sees

Shipping a schema change to a device that holds the only copy of a shop’s books is the most dangerous thing this app does. Version 2 added one index and shipped with a restore path that rebuilds bill counters from the restored bills themselves — because an earlier build could hand out a bill number twice after a restore, and two bills sharing a number is a discrepancy nobody can unpick at closing time.

Dark

Shops open before dawn and close after dark.

Not an inversion. The brand teal is too dark to read as text on a dark surface, so the role is re-cast in a lighter tone that carries the same meaning.

The receipt stays white in the dark. It is paper — a dark receipt would be a picture of something that cannot exist, and the shopkeeper is checking it against what the printer is about to produce.

First run

The one screen in the app that is not a till.

A colour per question, heavy display type, and ink drawn by hand. It stops at the front door.

A fresh install used to open on an empty billing screen — technically correct and completely unwelcoming. Three questions later the till carries the shop’s name, knows whether it charges GST, and has that shop’s fastest sellers waiting on the front screen.

Setup is the only moment in the product with no queue behind it, so it is the only place that can afford charm over density. The step you are on is the colour of the entire screen, so progress is felt before the rule at the top is read.

Measured, not chosen Ink clears 13:1 on all three grounds. The same arithmetic says a white field on the lime ground is 1.15:1 — so the input boxes are separated by their ink border, never by their fill.
Dark, not dropped The three hues get dark counterparts, because a first run at night should still feel like this screen. Every stroke in the drawings is currentColor, so the ink flips to cream with no second copy of the art.

What broke

Five defects that shipped, and what each one actually cost.

A case study without this section is a portfolio. Every one of these was found after it was written, and three of them were losing money.

Credit could not be selected at all

A polish commit left the credit panel behind a condition that could never be true, so every khata sale and every split payment silently became unreachable. The test suite stayed green because the logic lived in component state where nothing could assert on it. The fix was not the condition — it was moving every payment decision into a pure, tested module. Reintroducing the original defect now fails nine tests.

Repeat partial returns refunded more than the sale

Returning the same line twice was validated against the original sale each time instead of against everything already returned. Proven with a probe, not by reasoning: ₹50 refunded against a ₹30 sale, and stock going 100 → 102. Returns are now checked cumulatively, refund the credit portion before the cash, and get their own numbering series.

Two bills could share one number after a restore

Restoring a backup brought the bills back but not the counters that hand out their numbers, so the next sale reused one. In a shop’s books that is not a bug, it is a discrepancy nobody can unpick. Restore now rebuilds the counters from the restored bills and recalculates every customer balance afterwards.

Controls that were invisible — twice

A search field filled with the page colour, on the page colour. Then, after the palette changed, every neutral fill inside a sheet — because sheets sit on the page colour, not on the card colour. The same class of mistake, found the same way both times: by looking, not by reading the diff.

A 26-pixel field on the very first screen

The item-name box during setup was too narrow to show a placeholder or aim a thumb at, because a shared input hardcoded a full width that beat the width its neighbour asked for. Automated checks passed — a 26 px field still accepts keystrokes. It was reachable by code and unusable by a person, and only measuring it caught the difference.

Where it landed

A shop can install it, set it up in four minutes, and bill all day without a network.

What the design work actually was

Less drawing screens than deciding what the counter would tolerate: how big a number has to be, how few taps a weight can take, which warnings are worth interrupting for, and what happens to a half-finished bill when a phone dies.

What it taught

That a design system is not a colour palette — it is the set of decisions you can still defend a month later. Nineteen of them are written down here with the reasoning attached, including the ones that were wrong.

Still open

Backup is on-device and manual; an automatic off-device copy has to land before this holds a real shop’s only books. And the keyboard-first path has not yet been watched in the hands of an operator who did not design it.
