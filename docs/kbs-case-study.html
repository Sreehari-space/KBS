The shop

The counter is the design constraint

A kirana shop runs on three things the software had to respect rather than replace: a paper notebook of who owes what, a shopkeeper serving one customer while two more wait, and a phone that is often the cheapest Android in the shop.

Signal is intermittent. Power is intermittent. Neither is an edge case — they are Tuesday. So "works offline" could not be a feature bolted on at the end; it had to be the shape of the whole thing. There is no server. The device holds the only copy of the shop's books.

I need auto save for all the transactions, every bill and data should be stored. There is no compromise on this. The brief, verbatim

That sentence decided more of the design than any screen did. It ruled out a Save button, ruled out optimistic UI that shows a bill as done before it is on disk, and set the standard everything else was measured against.

Durability

D10 · D11 · D12

Storage is a design decision, not a technical one

The obvious answer for a small local app is the browser's simplest storage. We ran the arithmetic instead. It caps at about 5 MB — roughly six weeks of bills for a busy shop — and it rewrites the entire blob synchronously on every write, which freezes a low-end phone at the exact moment a customer is waiting.

So bills live in IndexedDB, and every sale commits as a single atomic transaction: the bill, the stock decrement, the ledger entry, the bill number and the cleared draft all land together or none of them do. The receipt renders only after that resolves. If the write fails the cart survives, so the shopkeeper can retry with the customer still standing there.

D11 A bill is never shown as complete unless it is on disk.

The receipt is a consequence of a successful write, not a prediction of one.

D12 Bills are never pruned and sales are immutable.

"Every bill stored" means forever. Corrections are new return bills, so an interrupted write can never corrupt history that already exists.

The interface consequence is quiet and constant: a small Saved state beside the running total. Never a toast — a toast implies an event, and this is just a fact. It flips to "Saving…" the instant the cart changes and back only once the write resolves.

Two scripts

Typography

A till that has to think in two alphabets

Shopkeepers type in Tamil, in English, and in Tanglish — Tamil words spelled in Latin letters. Typing thakkali should produce Tomato and தக்காளி; typing தக்காளி should fill the English back. That runs on an offline lexicon with a phonetic fallback, because a cloud translation call is useless in the exact conditions this app exists for.

The honesty problem was worth more than the cleverness. Transliteration converts sound, not meaning, so a guessed name is labelled as a sound-alike and asks to be checked. A wrong Tamil name printed on a bill is worse than a blank one.

Where the visual language had to bend

Modern app typography leans on tight negative tracking for headlines. Applied to Tamil it pulls the glyph clusters apart and reads as broken rather than as confident. So the display treatment tightens Latin and numerals and resets itself for Tamil:

CSS :lang(ta) .display { letter-spacing: 0; line-height: 1.25; }

Numerals keep the tight setting in both languages — which is what the style was actually for.

Money

D1

Money is typography before it is anything else

Every amount in the system is an integer count of paise. Floating point cannot represent a rupee exactly, and a till that is occasionally one paisa out is a till nobody trusts twice.

On screen that turns into a small, repeated decision: amounts are set tabular and slightly tight so a column of them scans as a column, and the paise are set smaller and lighter. ₹1,240.00 reads as "twelve forty" and the ".00" stops competing. Counts are deliberately not money — same tabular figures, none of the weight — so "12 items" never carries the visual force of "₹1,240".

One bill model feeds four renderers — the 58 mm thermal print, the WhatsApp text, a shareable image, and the raster the Bluetooth printer needs. They cannot drift, because there is only one of them. The Bluetooth path rasterises rather than sending text: cheap thermal printers carry no Tamil font in ROM, so a Tamil bill sent as characters prints as boxes.

The redesign

Reference → till

Borrowing a design language without borrowing a brand

The visual system was rebuilt against a modern banking app as reference. What transferred: pill controls with one bright fill reserved for the single primary action, borderless cards separated by the background gap rather than a border, sheets with a large top radius, and big tight-set numerals.

The signature move was translated rather than copied. In the reference a gradient sits behind the account balance at the top of the home screen. A till's equivalent number is the running total — and it belongs at the bottom, where the thumb already is.

Where a bank app is the wrong model

A banking app is read-mostly: you scroll, you tap occasionally, and generous whitespace costs you nothing. A till is hammered. The shopkeeper hits the same product tiles hundreds of times a day, one handed, at speed. Relaxing the grid to the reference's airy spacing would have halved how many products fit on screen and made the shop measurably slower for the sake of looking calmer. The density stayed.

#0F766E #2EE8C8 #062E29 #F4F5F4

The primary teal is #0F766E because a lighter, prettier teal measured 3.7:1 on white and fails for small text. The mint carries near-black ink at 10.4:1. The hero gradient starts at the primary so even small white type on its lightest band clears 4.5:1.

What broke

D13 – D18

The failures were more instructive than the features

Four of these were found by driving the built app rather than by reading the code, and two of them lose real money. They are here because the rules that came out of them are the most durable part of the design system.

A polish commit made every credit sale unreachable

Fixing "the payment sheet opens in a yellow warning state" gated the customer picker on a condition that could never be true. Tapping Credit did nothing — no highlight, no picker, the button simply dead. The deeper error was modelling credit as a payment; it is the absence of one, the remainder resolved at commit.

Cost: the flow a kirana shop runs on, silently gone.

Repeat partial returns refunded more than the bill was worth

Quantities were validated against the original line quantity rather than what was still un-returned. Returning 2 of 5, then 5 of 5, paid out ₹70 on a ₹50 sale and invented two units of stock.

Cost: real cash, out of the drawer.

Restoring a backup reissued live bill numbers

Bill counters were never exported and never rebuilt, so restoring onto a new device — the only case that matters — restarted the sequence. Two different sales, one number, no error. You would find out during a dispute, months later.

Cost: the integrity of the record itself.

Controls the same colour as the thing behind them — twice

A search field on a page fill identical to its own background. Then, after the palette changed, every neutral control inside a sheet, because sheets sit on the page colour so cards can float on them. Invisible, both times, and invisible in the diff too.

Cost: caught only by looking at rendered pixels.

The rules that came out of them

D18 Anything that decides whether a shop can put a bill on a khata is not presentation.

Payment logic moved out of component state into a tested pure module. Reintroducing the original bug now fails nine tests.

D15 A loading state and an empty state are different states.

Defaulting a live query to an empty array renders "no items found" for a frame on every mount. That single frame is most of what made screens feel cheap.

D16 Never report a number the data cannot support.

Profit counts only lines with a recorded cost price; the rest is shown separately. Treating a missing cost as zero would flatter the shop and make the figure useless.

Honest gaps

  • Private-browsing detection is a heuristic, not a fact — no browser exposes it. The real defence is the off-device backup nag, which is why it moved out of Settings and into the app shell where it is actually seen.
  • Backup is still a manual daily ritual attached to closing the shop. Automatic off-device backup is the next thing worth building.
  • The keyboard-first billing path is built and tested, but it has not yet been watched in the hands of someone billing at real speed.
